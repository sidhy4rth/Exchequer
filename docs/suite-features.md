# The investigation suite: nine features

Exchequer is growing from a tracer into a suite for crypto-fraud cases. Tracing
(blockchain analytics) is what Exchequer already is. These are the nine features
the team agreed to add around it, in the team's own numbering. Talk about work in
these terms: "feature 7", "the letter generator".

"Build" means we write it. "Integrate" means we import another tool's output.
"Link" means we point to the tool from the case and record what it produced.
Status is what exists in this repository as of 2026-11-14.

| # | Feature | What it does | Build, integrate or link | Status in the repo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Attribution data | Who owns an address | Build the India label set; import public lists (sanctions, Tether freezes, public tags) | Live: 68.4 million flagged addresses in the label store (release `labels-2026-11-14b`), read by `label_store.py`, `label_rules.py`, `tron_tags.py`, `never_flag.py`, `risk_matcher.py`. India exchange set: 175 wallets of WazirX, CoinDCX, CoinSwitch and Unocoin on Ethereum, Polygon, BSC, Arbitrum and Tron, each with its public source in `data/india_exchanges.json` (#135; round 2, #207, adds CoinSwitch's 2024-25 wallet lists and WazirX's archived proof-of-reserves page; round 3, #245, adds the CoinDCX list it says it published through Nansen Portfolio and Unocoin's June 2023 proof-of-reserves wallet). Left: keep adding lists; no public source yet for ZebPay, Bitbns, Giottus, Mudrex or BuyUcoin wallets |
| 2 | Mobile and computer forensics | Phones and PCs, including wallet apps and seed phrases | Integrate: import Cellebrite, Magnet AXIOM, MSAB XRY, Oxygen or Autopsy exports; search them for addresses, hashes and seed words | In progress (#145): slice 1 scans a text-based export (CSV, TSV, XML, JSON, HTML, text) in the browser for addresses of the traced chains, transaction hashes and seed-phrase runs (count and line only, never the words), on the Evidence tab behind `EXCHEQUER_EXPORT_SCAN`. No vendor-specific importers yet (no sample exports) |
| 3 | Memory and disk tools | RAM capture, encrypted volumes, password recovery | Link from the case; record the hashes of what they produced | Live: the exhibit register on the Evidence tab (`exhibits.py`, #103) records each file by its hashes, checks a file against them in the browser, and keeps the chain of custody |
| 4 | OSINT | Handles, domains, phone numbers, app and site infrastructure | Build light lookups tied to the case (domain, Telegram handle); link to the rest | Live: case identifiers (`identifiers.py`, #105), domain lookup (`domain_lookup.py`, #106, flag `EXCHEQUER_OSINT_LOOKUPS`, on in production since #108), links across cases (#107); review fixes for #111 in #115 (domain lookups for signed-in officers only) |
| 5 | Telecom and bank analysis | CDR, IPDR, bank statements | Build an importer that joins bank and exchange records to the trace | First slice built, behind the flag `EXCHEQUER_STATEMENT_IMPORT` (off; #144, `docs/handoff/bank-statement-import.md`): a bank or exchange statement CSV read in the browser (`statement.js`) and lined up with the case's payments, bank references, exchange names and on-chain times; the rows the officer ticks saved to the case (`statement_imports.py`). Not yet tried on a real statement. Second slice built behind `EXCHEQUER_CDR_IMPORT` (off; #167): a call detail record CSV read in the browser (`cdr.js`), top contacts, first and last contact, handsets (IMEI), the case's own phone identifiers and, for the numbers the officer picks, their other cases; a summary saved to the case (`cdr_imports.py`). Not yet tried on a real CDR. No IPDR yet |
| 6 | Case management and evidence locker | Cases, tasks, deadlines, exhibits, chain of custody, audit trail | Build: the core of the suite | Partly there: `accounts.py`, `auth.py`, `audit.py`, `evidence.py` (hashes every provider response), `signing.py`, `pdf_verify.py`, `case_refs.py`, `batches.py`, `correlation.py`. Since 13 Nov also the exhibit register (`exhibits.py`, #103) and the asset register's court deadlines (`assets.py`, #101). Since 13 Nov a draft of the s.63(4) certificate for a case's exhibits (`certificates.py`, #113, #114): Part A filled from the register, Part B left for the expert, as a PDF. Since 14 Nov also the s.63(4) certificate for Exchequer's own report (`certificates.py`, #119): the officer gives the report file, the server checks it as `/verify` does and states its SHA-256. Behind `EXCHEQUER_CASE_BUNDLE` (off), a case bundle (`case_bundle.py`, #186): one zip of the signed report, the registers as CSV and the audit trail, with a manifest of SHA-256 hashes. A task list per case (`tasks.py`): the server (#122: tasks with an assignee's name, a due date and their history, closed as done or dropped with a reason, nothing deleted), the panel on the Evidence tab and a "Tasks due" card on the home page (#123), and tasks suggested from the case's own records, such as a request with no reply or held funds with no court order (#124). |
| 7 | Request and letter generator | Notices to exchanges, banks, telecoms; foreign letters of request | Build | Partly there: `request_letters.py` (letters to exchanges), `freeze_requests.py` (tracks what was sent and what came of it). Bank and telecom notices since #99 (`notice_letters.py`). A draft letter of request to a foreign country (BNSS s.112) in the MHA's format, `letters_of_request.py` (#125); its notes say whether the MHA's 2019 treaty list (Figure 3.6) names the country, and it can be logged in the requests log as its own kind (#130, #136) |
| 8 | Government platforms | NCRP and 1930, CFCFRMS, Samanvaya, Sahyog, CCTNS | Link and import; we cannot write into them without an official integration | Partly there: NCRP numbers in complaints and case references (`complaints.py`, `case_refs.py`, `payment_intake.py`), and the citizen site's complaint intake. No imports from the other platforms |
| 9 | Custody wallet tooling | Government-controlled multisig wallets for seized crypto | Build the procedure and the records; never hold keys on our servers | Live: the asset register (`assets.py`, #101) records what was frozen or seized, who holds the keys (names only) and the 45-day court deadline, with a warning on the home page. Built behind `EXCHEQUER_CUSTODY_WALLETS` (off): a record of each multisig custody wallet (chain, address, scheme, m of n, signers by name; #129), its signing log of deposits and releases with distinct-signer approvals and the court order for a release (#139), a panel under the asset register (#140), and the officer's checklist `docs/custody-procedure.md`. Never a key on the server |

Background for each feature (what police use today, the legal powers involved,
and how an investigator moves through the suite) is in the team's report
"Exchequer Investigation Suite: tools, workflow and law". Ask your human for
the link.

How to switch on the features still behind flags, and check each on the live site first: `docs/flag-rollout.md`.

When a feature is claimed, its handoff file in `docs/handoff/` names it by
number, and this table's status column is updated in the same pull request.
