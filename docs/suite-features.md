# The investigation suite: nine features

Exchequer is growing from a tracer into a suite for crypto-fraud cases. Tracing
(blockchain analytics) is what Exchequer already is. These are the nine features
the team agreed to add around it, in the team's own numbering. Talk about work in
these terms: "feature 7", "the letter generator".

"Build" means we write it. "Integrate" means we import another tool's output.
"Link" means we point to the tool from the case and record what it produced.
Status is what exists on `main` as of 2026-11-15. "In review" means the pull requests are open and not on `main` yet.

| # | Feature | What it does | Build, integrate or link | Status in the repo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Attribution data | Who owns an address | Build the India label set; import public lists (sanctions, Tether freezes, public tags) | Live: 68.4 million flagged addresses in the label store (release `labels-2026-11-14b`), read by `label_store.py`, `label_rules.py`, `tron_tags.py`, `never_flag.py`, `risk_matcher.py`. India exchange set: 175 wallets of WazirX, CoinDCX, CoinSwitch and Unocoin on Ethereum, Polygon, BSC, Arbitrum and Tron, each with its public source in `data/india_exchanges.json` (#135; round 2, #207, adds CoinSwitch's 2024-25 wallet lists and WazirX's archived proof-of-reserves page; round 3, #247, adds the CoinDCX list it says it published through Nansen Portfolio and Unocoin's June 2023 proof-of-reserves wallet). Exchange wallets from the exchanges' own proof-of-reserves lists: Bybit on Ethereum, BSC, Polygon and Arbitrum and OKX on Polygon and Arbitrum (#226), 51 Tron wallets of Bybit, HTX, Binance and KuCoin (#221), and the two Ethereum wallets OKX sweeps USDT and USDC into (#248). The rest of OKX's Ethereum and Tron wallets wait for the label store: the code that reads exchange rows from the store is on `main` behind `EXCHEQUER_STORE_EXCHANGE_ROWS` (off; #223, ADR-0011), and the store release that carries them is not published. Ethereum exchange labels on proxy or forwarder contracts are typed `contract_wallet`, keeping their old type as a role so no trace's conclusion changes (#234, with #238). Three older Ethereum contract wallets (Kraken 5, Coinbase 10, Gemini 2) carry the role `hot_wallet`, measured keylessly on chain as where deposit addresses sweep (#253); the other 78 role-less contract wallets stay so, each with its reason in `tests/fixtures/contract_wallet_roles.json`. Behind `EXCHEQUER_PRIVACY_CATEGORY` (off), privacy protocols (Railgun, Aztec, Hinkal, Privacy Pools) shown on a trace as a note, never a risk hit or a stop (merged with #149). Left: keep adding lists; no public source yet for ZebPay, Bitbns, Giottus, Mudrex or BuyUcoin wallets |
| 2 | Mobile and computer forensics | Phones and PCs, including wallet apps and seed phrases | Integrate: import Cellebrite, Magnet AXIOM, MSAB XRY, Oxygen or Autopsy exports; search them for addresses, hashes and seed words | On `main` behind `EXCHEQUER_EXPORT_SCAN` (off; #147, with #154, #155, #156 and #164 merged with it): on the Evidence tab, a text-based export (CSV, TSV, XML, JSON, HTML, text) scanned in the browser for addresses of the traced chains, transaction hashes and seed-phrase runs (count and line only, never the words); the server records only that a scan ran and its counts (`routes/export_scan.py`); a found address can be added to the case as a wallet identifier (#164). Not yet tried on a real export. No vendor-specific importer (no sample exports) |
| 3 | Memory and disk tools | RAM capture, encrypted volumes, password recovery | Link from the case; record the hashes of what they produced | Live: the exhibit register on the Evidence tab (`exhibits.py`, #103) records each file by its hashes, checks a file against them in the browser, and keeps the chain of custody |
| 4 | OSINT | Handles, domains, phone numbers, app and site infrastructure | Build light lookups tied to the case (domain, Telegram handle); link to the rest | Live: case identifiers (`identifiers.py`, #105), domain lookup (`domain_lookup.py`, #106, flag `EXCHEQUER_OSINT_LOOKUPS`, on in production since #108), links across cases (#107); review fixes for #111 in #115 (domain lookups for signed-in officers only). On `main` behind `EXCHEQUER_TELEGRAM_LOOKUPS` (off; #162, with #169 and #170 merged with it): a Telegram username's public t.me page (`telegram_lookup.py`), read once and kept with its SHA-256, for signed-in officers only; it also needs `EXCHEQUER_OSINT_LOOKUPS` on |
| 5 | Telecom and bank analysis | CDR, IPDR, bank statements | Build an importer that joins bank and exchange records to the trace | On `main` behind two flags, both off. `EXCHEQUER_STATEMENT_IMPORT` (#146, with #151, #152 and #153 merged with it; `docs/handoff/bank-statement-import.md`): a bank or exchange statement CSV read in the browser (`statement.js`) and lined up with the case's payments, bank references, exchange names and on-chain times; the rows the officer ticks saved to the case (`statement_imports.py`). `EXCHEQUER_CDR_IMPORT` (#168, #172 and #173, merged with #146): a call detail record CSV read in the browser (`cdr.js`), top contacts, first and last contact, handsets (IMEI), the case's own phone identifiers and, for the numbers the officer picks, their other cases; a summary saved to the case (`cdr_imports.py`). A saved statement or CDR import entered by mistake can be withdrawn with a reason, never deleted (#183, #184; migration `g7_statements_2`). Not yet tried on a real statement or CDR. No IPDR yet |
| 6 | Case management and evidence locker | Cases, tasks, deadlines, exhibits, chain of custody, audit trail | Build: the core of the suite | Partly there (live unless a flag is named): `accounts.py`, `auth.py`, `audit.py`, `evidence.py` (hashes every provider response), `signing.py`, `pdf_verify.py`, `case_refs.py`, `batches.py`, `correlation.py`. Since 13 Nov also the exhibit register (`exhibits.py`, #103) and the asset register's court deadlines (`assets.py`, #101). Since 13 Nov a draft of the s.63(4) certificate for a case's exhibits (`certificates.py`, #113, #114): Part A filled from the register, Part B left for the expert, as a PDF. Since 14 Nov also the s.63(4) certificate for Exchequer's own report (`certificates.py`, #119): the officer gives the report file, the server checks it as `/verify` does and states its SHA-256. Behind `EXCHEQUER_CASE_BUNDLE` (off), a case bundle (`case_bundle.py`, #187, #190): one zip of the signed report, the registers as CSV and the audit trail, with a manifest of SHA-256 hashes; since #244 it also holds the task list, and, where their flags are on, the custody wallets and signing log, the statement rows and the CDR summaries. Live with no flag, a task list per case (`tasks.py`): the server (#122: tasks with an assignee's name, a due date and their history, closed as done or dropped with a reason, nothing deleted), the panel on the Evidence tab and a "Tasks due" card on the home page (#123), and tasks suggested from the case's own records, such as a request with no reply or held funds with no court order (#124). |
| 7 | Request and letter generator | Notices to exchanges, banks, telecoms; foreign letters of request | Build | Partly there: `request_letters.py` (letters to exchanges), `freeze_requests.py` (tracks what was sent and what came of it). Bank and telecom notices since #99 (`notice_letters.py`). A draft letter of request to a foreign country (BNSS s.112) in the MHA's format, `letters_of_request.py` (#125); its notes say whether the MHA's 2019 treaty list (Figure 3.6) names the country, and it can be logged in the requests log as its own kind (#130, #136), followed up after 30 days rather than 7 (#158). A bank hold's sign-off covers only the exact details approved (#133) |
| 8 | Government platforms | NCRP and 1930, CFCFRMS, Samanvaya, Sahyog, CCTNS | Link and import; we cannot write into them without an official integration | Partly there: NCRP numbers in complaints and case references (`complaints.py`, `case_refs.py`, `payment_intake.py`), and the citizen site's complaint intake. Since 15 Nov (#218) a soft note when an NCRP number is not the CFCFRMS SOP's usual 14 digits starting 2 or 3 (`ncrp.py`; never a refusal). On `main` behind `EXCHEQUER_SOP_SUMMARY` (off; #219, #220, merged with #218): a copy-ready crypto summary of a case in the SOP's 9.4 terms (`sop_summary.py`), for the officer to enter on CFCFRMS or attach. On `main` behind `EXCHEQUER_SOP_DEADLINES` (off; #230): CFCFRMS SOP time limits shown as quoted reminders on bank holds in the asset register (`sop_reminders.py`). No published route exists for a tool to read from or write to NCRP, CFCFRMS, Samanvaya, Sahyog or CCTNS; findings and owner questions in `docs/handoff/government-platforms.md` |
| 9 | Custody wallet tooling | Government-controlled multisig wallets for seized crypto | Build the procedure and the records; never hold keys on our servers | Live: the asset register (`assets.py`, #101) records what was frozen or seized, who holds the keys (names only) and the 45-day court deadline, with a warning on the home page. On `main` behind `EXCHEQUER_CUSTODY_WALLETS` (off; #129, with #139, #140 and #142 merged with it): a record of each multisig custody wallet (chain, address, scheme, m of n, signers by name; `custody.py`), its signing log of deposits and releases with distinct-signer approvals and the court order for a release (`custody_log.py`), a panel under the asset register on the Letters tab, and the officer's checklist `docs/custody-procedure.md`. Never a key on the server |

A plain-English guide for officers, by task, is in `docs/officer-guide.md`.

Background for each feature (what police use today, the legal powers involved,
and how an investigator moves through the suite) is in the team's report
"Exchequer Investigation Suite: tools, workflow and law". Ask your human for
the link.

How to switch on the features still behind flags, and check each on the live site first: `docs/flag-rollout.md`.

When a feature is claimed, its handoff file in `docs/handoff/` names it by
number, and this table's status column is updated in the same pull request.
