Phases 0–2 · from an idea to a question

PHASE 0 · THE IDEA · UP TO 4 SEP

SIH26183 asks what a police officer should do with a victim’s wallet address. The answer the team chose was a stance before it was code: no model, no clustering, no proprietary score — a conclusion that reaches a courtroom must be one an officer can re-check by hand. The one-pager of 4 September laid it out as six steps.

First live result: a reported address traced to Binance 14 in 2 hops, confidence 0.80, 138 addresses mapped. 337 verified wallets, 18 exchanges, Ethereum only.

PHASE 1 · THE FOUNDATION · 5–7 SEP

The first commit traced three chains from day one, because Etherscan’s free tier serves Ethereum mainnet only. One asset per trace — amounts in different coins cannot be compared or scored.

Ethereumvia Etherscan
BNB Smart Chainvia NodeReal
Tronvia TronGrid

By 7 September: a two-sided test suite (every rule must fire on its shape and stay silent on ordinary activity), reverse tracing to find the other people who paid a scammer, and 407 addresses from OFAC’s live sanctions list. The mixer list came back empty — Tornado Cash had been delisted — and that was written down as the correct answer.

PHASE 2 · THE INTERNAL JUDGING ROUND · BETWEEN 7 AND 15 SEP

“Since you pointed out money laundering, have you done any research to support your claims?”

The honest answer was no. The README asserted that laundering moves as stablecoins, that USDT on Tron is the dominant rail for Indian scam proceeds, and that its thresholds were right — and cited nothing. The same judges would sit the next round. The exact date of this round is not recorded in the repository.
